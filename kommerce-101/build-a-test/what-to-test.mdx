A test is a question with money behind it. Before anyone writes copy, five things get decided.

| Decision | The rule |
| --- | --- |
| **Persona** | Exactly one. Not a blend. |
| **Awareness** | Exactly one of five levels. |
| **Angle** | The claim you are arguing. |
| **Variable** | The one thing that differs between versions. |
| **Mode** | Static, video, motion or creator. From evidence, not taste. |

Get these wrong and everything downstream is wasted work. Beautiful ads that test nothing are the most expensive thing an account produces.

## Awareness decides your first line

How much the reader already knows decides what your headline is allowed to say. An ad opening on a discount speaks to someone already choosing between brands. Show it to someone who does not know she has the problem and it is noise.

The reverse fails too. A long story about recognising a feeling wastes a reader who already has her card out.

<AwarenessPicker />

Most accounts are thick with Most Aware ads, because retargeting fills that in by itself. The gap is almost always higher up the funnel.

<Warning>
  Selecting several awareness levels in one test multiplies your output and your budget. Each level
  is a separate idea and needs its own spend.
</Warning>

## One variable, or no answer

If two things changed, the verdict cannot tell you which one moved the number. You spent the money and learned nothing.

The harder trap is the unfair test. A test is only readable if both sides got a real chance.

<FairTestCheck />

The third rule is the one people break without noticing. If you believe in version A, it is easy to build B carelessly. B loses, and you conclude the idea was wrong when the execution was wrong. That mistake then goes into the account's learnings and misleads every test after it.
