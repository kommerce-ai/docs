The brief says what the ad argues. Generation builds it. Each ad needs a scene decided first: shot type, setting, props, light, backdrop and the copy zone, plus where the product sits and how much of the frame it fills.

{/* Add a walkthrough here once one is recorded: <Walkthrough id="..." title="Generating and reviewing a set of statics" /> */}

## Product truth overrides the brief

The model gets physical facts wrong unless the scene stops it.

Models size a product to the hand holding it. A 25 mm pendant comes out at 35 to 50 mm in a tight hand shot, whatever the words say. For anything small, frame waist-up or wider and put a known object in the shot.

If the identity lives on the label, use a front-facing close shot with the label readable. If there are several variants, use a lineup from a real group photo, because generated lineups drift in shape.

## Meta covers part of every frame

Text outside the safe area gets covered or cropped. These are not guidelines, they are where the interface physically sits.

<SafeZones />

## Three stops, no fourth

The eye should move in three beats. The product stops the scroll, the headline says the idea, and the offer gives a reason to act. Beat three sits nearest Meta's button, so the read ends at the tap.

Each beat lands a clear step quieter than the last, roughly half the size or weight. Two elements at the same weight fight, and the eye stalls on neither.

## Count the cost before you confirm

Asking to generate returns a price and spends nothing. It runs only when you confirm.

Work the count out first. It is roughly angles × products × awareness levels × personas × variations. Three angles across three awareness levels, two personas and three variations is 54 assets from one click.

<Warning>
  The wallet is a hard ceiling. A run that would exceed the balance is refused outright. It is never
  half-completed.
</Warning>

## Reviewing what came back

Zoom in before judging. Text that looks wrong at thumbnail size is often fine close up.

Then comment on the region at fault rather than describing the problem in general terms. A regeneration that knows where the problem is produces a targeted fix. One that only knows "make it better" rerolls the whole asset. Regeneration replaces the asset in place, and there is no undo.

<Note>
  For a matched pair, both versions use the same image and differ only in the variable's words. That
  is what makes the result attributable to the thing you were testing.
</Note>
