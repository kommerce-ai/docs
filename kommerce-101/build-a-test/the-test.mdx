The brief says what you want to learn. The test is how Kommerce runs it.

Before creating anything, ask Kommerce for the test idea. It returns the bar, the budget and the stop rule. Use those numbers in the plan rather than inventing your own. You tell it the goal, the persona, the awareness level and how many versions you want.

## Versions are ideas, and ideas cost money

Each version gets its own ad set and its own budget. Different executions of one version share that budget.

So five executions of one angle cost one budget. Five angles cost five. This is the thing people get wrong when they scope a test, and it is why a test plan grows expensive without anyone deciding that it should.

<TestDesigner />

## The budget trap

Set lifetime budgets per ad set. Never a shared campaign budget.

Meta moves money toward whatever looks best early. That is Meta doing its job well, and it is exactly wrong for a test. You are buying information. Meta is buying results. A shared budget starves one side before it has produced a verdict, and you pay for an answer you never get.

## Strictness

Cautious, balanced or bold. It sets how much evidence Kommerce wants before it acts.

Bold calls things sooner and is wrong more often. Cautious spends more before deciding. Pick bold when creative is cheap and you want throughput. Pick cautious when each test costs real money to produce, or when the client will ask you to defend the call.

## The order of operations

<Steps>
  <Step title="Check the test idea">
    Get the bar, budget and stop rule before writing them into the plan.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Show the versions, budget and bar to whoever owns the money">
    This is a decision, not a formality.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Create the test">
    It is a draft. Nothing is running.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Write a brief per version">
    Each one carries what it says and what it shows.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Stop there">
    Generating creative and publishing are separate, deliberate steps.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  Kommerce stores one persona and one awareness level per test. If a test genuinely needs two, split
  it into two tests, or record the primary one and note the second in the hypothesis.
</Note>
