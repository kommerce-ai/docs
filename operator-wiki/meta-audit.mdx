The Meta audit is a diagnosis, not a discussion. It pulls the account's real numbers, grades four dimensions, and hands you a ranked action list where every claim carries a figure from the account.

Open **Meta Audit** at `/meta-audit` and pick the brand.

<Info>
  **The audit never modifies anything.** It cannot create, update, pause, or activate. If it recommends an action, it tells you which surface to do it from — you make the change yourself.
</Info>

## The four dimensions

They are graded in a fixed order, because upstream problems invalidate downstream conclusions.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Signal — is the tracking trustworthy?">
    Pixel freshness and event quality. If Signal is Red, **every number below it is suspect** and the audit will say so.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Structure — is budget consolidated enough to learn?">
    Fragmentation across ad sets, budget mode, and whether the account shape supports scale.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Waste — where is spend going that shouldn't?">
    Spend sitting below the kill line past the learning window, zombie campaigns, abandoned winners.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Creative — is the pool fresh and diverse?">
    Ad age, share of spend on stale creative, frequency, and hook rate.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Each dimension gets a grade — **Green**, **Yellow**, **Red**, or **Blind** — and a one-line receipt.

A receipt is a number, not a characterisation:

> Waste — RED — $18,400 of last-90d spend sat below 1.3 ROAS past day 3, across 4 campaigns.

**Blind** means the data for that dimension came back degraded. A verdict from partial data is legitimate; the audit will tell you what it could not see rather than guessing.

## The ranked actions

Below the scorecard sit up to five actions, ranked by expected impact. Each carries:

- **Why** — the receipt that justifies it
- **Expected** — the delta, tied to the number it came from

Two rules govern the ranking, and they matter when you are deciding what to do Monday morning:

1. **A downstream fix never outranks an upstream one that invalidates it.** A broken pixel outranks a creative rotation even when the creative delta looks larger, because the creative delta is measured against fiction.
2. **One change per problem.** Each action is a hypothesis. Running two at once makes the cause unrecoverable.

Fewer than five real findings means fewer than five actions. The audit does not pad.

## Asking follow-ups

The audit is chat-first. Three starters are offered:

- *What's my biggest leak?*
- *Which action should I approve first?*
- *Where is my budget being wasted?*

Ask it to explain a receipt, to justify a ranking, or to tell you what it could not measure.

## Account type

The audit judges eCom and lead-gen accounts differently:

| Account type | Judged on | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **eCom** | ROAS against the kill line | Campaigns under the spend floor are ignored — a 9× ROAS on $40 is noise, not signal |
| **Lead-gen** | Cost per lead | ROAS is absent or meaningless and will not be reported |

Type is detected automatically, and the audit states which mode it used.

## Known limits

These are stated by the audit itself when relevant, and they are worth knowing before you act:

- **EMQ and CAPI status cannot be read** through the Meta API. Both need manual confirmation in Events Manager, and Signal is graded accordingly.
- **Ad sets in the learning phase are never evaluated.** They are flagged and excluded from every conclusion.
- **Meta insights truncate at roughly 37 months.** A "maximum" monthly series is not necessarily account lifetime.
- **Attribution is Meta's own.** If the brand has Shopify or GA4 connected and Meta-reported purchases diverge from the store by more than ~25%, that gap is itself a hard stop — and reconciling it is outside the audit's scope.

<Warning>
  Run the audit **before** you spend on a new account, not after. Its most common Red is Signal, and every optimisation you make on top of broken tracking is wasted work.
</Warning>

## Re-running

The audit caches its last result per brand. Force a fresh run when the account has materially changed — after a tracking fix, a restructure, or a significant budget shift.
