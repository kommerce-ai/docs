---
title: "Goals and economics"
description: "Turning what a client wants into a bar a test can be judged against, and what that bar costs to run."
---

Clients state goals in revenue. Tests are judged in cost per order. Translating between the two is the first real delivery task.

## Ask for the number behind the number

"We want to grow" is not actionable.

| Ask | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Target monthly spend | Decides how many ideas can run at once |
| Target cost per order, or target ROAS | Becomes the goal |
| What is constrained | Stock, fulfilment, cash. A winner you cannot fulfil is a problem |
| What already works | Where to start iterating instead of exploring |

## Target is not break-even

Break-even CPA is where an order stops making money. The client's target is lower. That gap is the margin they want.

Give Kommerce the break-even number. Setting the bar at the target makes the engine kill ideas that were profitable but not yet excellent, and those are your best iteration material.

Hold the target separately as the commercial goal. The bar decides what survives. The target decides whether you are happy.

## Budget decides how much you can learn

Each idea needs about two and a half times break-even CPA to produce a verdict. Kommerce reserves enough on top to confirm whatever passes.

At a $40 break-even, each idea costs about $100 to read.

| | Multiple | At $40 break-even |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Funding per idea | 2.5× | $100 |
| Pause considered | 2× | $80 |
| Kill considered | 3× | $120 |
| Proving a winner | up to 10× | $400 |

A client wanting ten ideas tested this month is describing a budget, whether they know it or not.

<Note>
  When budget is tight, fewer ideas with enough spend behind each beats more ideas sharing a budget too thin to produce a verdict on any of them. Say this early.
</Note>
