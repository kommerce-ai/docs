## What takes how long

| Work | Lead time | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Statics** | Fastest | Generated or designed from existing assets |
| **Motion** | Medium | Built by a designer from stills and cutouts |
| **Video edit** | Medium | Re-cutting footage that already exists |
| **Creator video** | Slowest | Casting, shipping product, filming |

Under about five days to launch, statics or motion are your realistic options. Creator content needs genuine runway — offer it when the timeline supports it and say how much time it needs.

## Test timelines

Separate from production. Once live:

- Nothing is judged for **72 hours**
- A test window runs about **6 days**
- Proving a winner properly takes roughly **15 orders**

<Warning>
  A client asking for results on day two is asking for noise. The engine deliberately withholds judgement while Meta is still learning, and reading early numbers as signal is how good ideas get killed.
</Warning>

## Quality standards

Before anything publishes:

<Steps>
  <Step title="One variable per idea">
    If an idea changes the angle and the audience and the format, the verdict tells you nothing you can act on.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Every claim carries evidence">
    Real numbers with spend attached. "Converts well" isn't evidence; "4 purchases at $32.08" is.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Claims are within brand rules">
    Approved claims verbatim, banned words absent. Non-negotiable in regulated categories.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Copy matches the awareness level">
    If the opening line doesn't match the awareness the idea is labelled with, either the copy or the label is wrong.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Ad copy limits

Meta primary text has a hard limit of 400 characters, and aim for 125 or under — past that it truncates and the reader sees "... See more" instead of your offer.
