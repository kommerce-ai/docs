## The three gates

| Gate | What happens | Who |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Generation quote** | A price is returned; nothing is spent until confirmed | Whoever owns the budget |
| **Creative review** | Each asset approved or rejected | Whoever owns creative sign-off |
| **Activation** | Paused ads are switched on in Ads Manager | Whoever owns spend |

Publishing never spends. That's a property of the system, not a convention — so "it went live by accident" isn't a failure mode here.

## Giving feedback that can be acted on

When rejecting an asset, **mark the region at fault** rather than describing the problem in general terms.

<Note>
  A regeneration that knows *where* the problem is produces a targeted fix. One that only knows "make it better" rerolls the whole asset — and you lose the parts that were working.
</Note>

Score it too. Scores accumulate into the account's picture of what works.

<Warning>
  Regeneration replaces an asset in place. There's no version history and no undo — download anything you might want back before you regenerate it.
</Warning>

## Briefs and sign-off

Generating from a brief **is** the sign-off — there's no separate approval step. Approving a brief ahead of production, or rejecting it outright, is a separate deliberate action.

A rejected brief stays rejected even if something gets generated from it later.

## What gets recorded

Every action is attributed — to a person, to Kenny, or to the system — with the brand and the outcome. This is what makes "who approved this claim" answerable weeks later, which in a regulated category is the difference between a conversation and a problem.
