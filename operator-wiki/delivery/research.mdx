Research exists to answer one question: what is worth testing next, and why do we believe it?

## What the account already knows

Read these before looking outward.

| Source | What it gives you |
| --- | --- |
| **Learnings** | What concluded tests established, with how confident each is |
| **Patterns** | Every ad grouped by persona, awareness, angle and page |
| **Past tests** | Questions already answered. Do not pay for them twice |
| **Account numbers** | Spend, ROAS, AOV, CTR, orders over a window |
| **Audit findings** | Structural or tracking problems underneath everything else |

Pattern groups are labelled **Winning**, **Front-runner**, **Promising**, **Losing** or **Too early**. These are chances, not proof. "Too early" means not enough data yet, not failing.

## Competitor research

Look for what competitors repeat and what they keep running. An ad that has run a long time is usually working.

A competitor pattern counts as evidence only if several brands repeat it, or one brand keeps an ad running a long time. One competitor doing something once is not a signal.

<Warning>
  Competitor research is billed per ad returned. The number of results you ask for is the price. Asking for a large sample to browse costs real money.
</Warning>

## Customer research

Copy written from instinct reads like copy. Copy written from customers' words reads like recognition.

| Source | What you get |
| --- | --- |
| Product review themes | The richest source. Real phrasing, real objections |
| Persona pain and objections | Structured, but a hypothesis until validated |
| Winning ad copy | What already worked here |
| Competitor wording | Patterns to borrow, phrasing to avoid |
| Category reviews on the web | Useful when the brand's own reviews are thin |

Copy exact phrases. Sort them into pain, desired outcome, objection and rave. Star the vivid ones. Those become headline candidates.

Never quote a review as a testimonial unless it is real, attributable, and the brand can produce it.

## Personas

A persona is a hypothesis about who buys and why. Every brief is written against exactly one.

A persona from a brand questionnaire is unvalidated. Say so when you cite it. A persona confirmed by review language or test results is evidence. The difference matters when deciding how much budget an idea deserves.

Be specific about who actually buys, not who the brand wishes bought.

## Evidence rules

Every claim that reaches a brief carries a tag saying where it came from. A line with no tag does not belong in a hypothesis.

Quote real numbers with spend attached. "4 purchases at $32.08 on $128 spend" is evidence. "Converts well" is not.

Under about ten orders, treat anything as directional.

An idea with no supporting evidence can still be worth testing. Label it exploratory and keep it to the minimum budget.
