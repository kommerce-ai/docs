---
title: "Understanding growth goals"
description: "Turning what a client says they want into something a test can be judged against."
---

Clients state goals in revenue. Tests are judged in cost per order. Translating between the two is the first real delivery task.

## Ask for the number behind the number

"We want to grow" isn't actionable. What you need:

| Ask | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Target monthly spend | Decides how many ideas can run at once |
| Target cost per order, or target ROAS | Becomes the bar |
| What's constrained | Stock, fulfilment, cash. A winner you can't fulfil is a problem, not a win |
| What's already working | Where to start iterating instead of exploring |

## Convert the goal into a bar

Break-even CPA is where an order stops making money. The client's *target* CPA is usually lower — that's the margin they want, not the point of failure.

<Warning>
  Give Kommerce the **break-even** number, not the target. Setting the bar at the target makes the engine kill ideas that were profitable but not yet excellent, and you'll throw away your best iteration material.
</Warning>

Hold the target separately as the commercial goal. The bar decides what survives; the target decides whether you're happy.

## Budget decides testing throughput

Each idea needs roughly two and a half times break-even CPA to produce a verdict, and Kommerce reserves enough on top to confirm whatever passes.

So at a $40 break-even, each idea costs about $100 to read. A client wanting ten ideas tested this month is describing a budget, whether they know it or not.

<Tip>
  When budget is tight, fewer ideas with enough spend behind each beats more ideas sharing a budget too thin to produce a verdict on any of them. Say this early — it's the most common place expectations and arithmetic diverge.
</Tip>
