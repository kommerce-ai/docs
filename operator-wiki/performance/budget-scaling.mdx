---
title: "Budget scaling and monitoring"
description: "Raising spend on a winner without breaking the thing that won."
---

Scaling is a series of small changes with checks between them, not one decision.

## Raise in steps

Change one dimension at a time and let each settle:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Raise budget on what already exists">
    Same ad set, same audience. The cheapest move and the most likely to hold.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Let it stabilise">
    A budget change can re-enter the learning phase. Judge nothing immediately.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Then widen the audience">
    Once the higher budget is holding.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Then add placements or formats">
    Each is a new context — treat a format change as an iteration worth briefing.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Warning>
  A winner was proven in a specific context: its ad set, audience, budget level and placements. Change several at once while raising spend and you're no longer running the thing that won.
</Warning>

## Watch for

| Signal | Likely cause |
| --- | --- |
| Cost per order climbs as budget rises | Audience saturating, or you outran what the creative supports |
| CPM climbs sharply | Competing harder for the same people |
| Frequency climbs | Same people seeing it repeatedly — creative fatigue follows |
| Account-wide cost per order drifts up | The brake is about to come on |

## The shared-budget trap

Raising budget on one ad set inside a campaign with a **shared budget** re-divides money across everything in that campaign.

<Warning>
  On a shared-budget campaign, "scale the winner" and "starve everything else" are the same action — whether or not anyone intended the second half.

  Say which campaign a winner sits in, and whether its budget is shared, before touching it.
</Warning>

## Creative fatigue

A winner doesn't stay a winner. Frequency climbs, response falls, and the cost per order drifts back toward the bar.

The answer is the next iteration of the winning idea, which is why **iterate** sends work back into testing rather than ending it. Scaling and testing run at the same time, permanently.

<Tip>
  Start the next iteration while the current winner is still working. By the time fatigue shows in the numbers, you're already behind.
</Tip>
