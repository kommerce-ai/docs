Kommerce doesn't say "this ad is good". It says how likely an idea is to beat break-even, given what it has seen so far. That's a more honest statement, and it behaves better on small samples.

## Why chances, not thresholds

A simple rule — "kill anything above 3× break-even" — ignores how much evidence there is. Two orders and two hundred orders get treated the same, and that's how good ideas die young.

Working in probabilities means the number carries its own confidence. An idea at 55% after heavy spend is genuinely mediocre. The same 55% after two orders means *we don't know yet*.

<Note>
  This is why the engine will keep funding something that looks unremarkable, and stop something that looks fine. It's reading the strength of the evidence, not just the average.
</Note>

## The numbers that matter

| Reading | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **90%** likely to beat break-even | Passes its first run |
| Holds up on fresh spend | Becomes a winner |
| Drops below a floor while confirming | Goes back to testing |
| Still ~50% after its funding | Promising, unproven — not a failure |

## Why a winner has to win twice

An idea that passes goes into a **second, separate run on fresh spend**.

A handful of early sales can make a mediocre idea look excellent. Something that got lucky passes the first round and then fails the second — and goes back to testing rather than to scale.

By the time Kommerce says **Winner: move it to scale**, the idea has been right twice, on different money.

## Ideas that earn more funding

If an idea's cost per order is already under break-even, it's making money while it proves itself. Funding it isn't waste, so Kommerce keeps extending it — up to ten times break-even — while it stays likely enough.

Proving a winner properly takes roughly 15 orders. A fixed budget would stop a genuinely good ad set before it got there.

## Patterns use the same language

Patterns group every ad in the account by persona, awareness, angle and page, and label each group:

**Winning · Front-runner · Promising · Losing · Too early**

<Warning>
  These are chances, not proof. "Too early" means not enough data yet — not that something is failing. Reading it as a negative is the most common misread of the pattern view.
</Warning>
