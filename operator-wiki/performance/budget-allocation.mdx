## What an idea costs to read

Each idea gets about **two and a half times break-even CPA** to produce a verdict. Kommerce also reserves enough to *confirm* whatever passes — so it will refuse to launch an idea it couldn't afford to prove.

At a $40 break-even:

| | Multiple | Amount |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Funding per idea | 2.5× | $100 |
| Pause considered | 2× | $80 |
| Kill considered | 3× | $120 |
| Proving a winner | up to 10× | $400 |

<Tip>
  That reservation is a feature. Launching five ideas you can only afford to half-prove produces five unreadable results and no winners.
</Tip>

## Exploit before you explore

Roughly **four out of five** new slots go to variations of ideas already passing or promising. One in five goes to genuinely new concepts.

The most common way testing goes wrong is abandoning something that was working to chase a new idea, then never getting enough evidence on either.

<Note>
  You can override the split for a brand where it doesn't fit. Do it knowingly — squeezing more out of a half-proven winner usually beats a fresh unknown.
</Note>

## Promising ideas are the cheapest wins available

An idea that ran out of funding while still looking good is recorded as **promising, unproven**. It is not a failure.

These are your best retest candidates: you've already paid for most of the evidence. Retesting one with proper funding is usually cheaper than finding a new idea from scratch.

## The account brake

Kommerce watches the whole account, not just your test, and pulls back when CPMs run at twice the account's normal level, or account-wide cost per order sits above one and a half times break-even for three days.

<Warning>
  If the brake comes on, the account is in trouble — not your test. Chasing individual ideas while it's on wastes your time. Find out what changed at account level first.
</Warning>
