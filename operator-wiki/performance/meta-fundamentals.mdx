You don't need to be a Meta expert to run Kommerce, but a few structural facts decide whether your tests mean anything.

## The hierarchy

| Level | Holds | Decides |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Campaign** | Ad sets | Objective, and budget if shared |
| **Ad set** | Ads | Audience, placements, and budget if per-ad-set |
| **Ad** | Creative and copy | What people actually see |

In Kommerce, **one idea equals one ad set**. Different executions of that idea are ads inside it, sharing its budget. That's why ideas cost money and versions don't.

## Budget: shared or per-ad-set

The single most consequential setting in a test.

**Per ad set** gives each idea its own money. Each gets a fair read. **Default to this for testing.**

**Shared across the campaign** lets Meta move budget to whatever looks best early.

<Warning>
  A shared budget will starve one side of a test before it produces a verdict. Meta is optimising for immediate return; you're buying information. Those goals conflict, and Meta wins.

  It's the right tool for scaling and the wrong one for testing.
</Warning>

## The learning phase

Meta needs conversions before delivery stabilises. Until then, performance swings for reasons that have nothing to do with your creative.

This is why **nothing is judged for 72 hours**. Early numbers are noise, and acting on them kills good ideas.

## Why tracking quality decides everything

Meta optimises toward the conversions it can see. Bad signal means bad optimisation and bad reporting, in the same direction, invisibly.

<Note>
  When purchase tracking is unreliable, Kommerce stops drawing conclusions from the checkout and purchase steps — it measures tracking, not ads. That's why a clean [account audit](/operator-wiki/client-success/account-audit) makes every later verdict more trustworthy.
</Note>

## Publishing is not spending

Everything Kommerce publishes arrives **paused**. Someone activates it in Ads Manager deliberately. There is no path where a test starts spending because a button was clicked by accident.
