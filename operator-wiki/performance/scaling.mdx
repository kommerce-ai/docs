---
title: "Scaling winners"
description: "Putting real budget behind a proven idea without breaking what made it work."
---

Scaling is what testing is for. When an idea survives both its runs, Kommerce says so plainly: **Winner: move it to scale**.

## What winner means

A winner has cleared a specific bar.

- It beat break-even CPA in its first run, with the evidence to back it
- It held up again on a separate run with fresh spend
- It did both over at least 72 hours with real budget behind it

That second run is what makes a winner trustworthy. An idea that got lucky early passes the first round and fails the second.

## Raise in steps

Change one dimension at a time and let each settle.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Raise budget on what exists">
    Same ad set, same audience. The cheapest move and the most likely to hold.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Let it stabilise">
    A budget change can re-enter the learning phase. Judge nothing immediately.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Then widen the audience">
    Once the higher budget is holding.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Then add placements or formats">
    Each is a new context. Treat a format change as an iteration worth briefing.
  </Step>
</Steps>

A winner was proven in a specific context: its ad set, audience, budget level and placements. Change several at once while raising spend and you are no longer running the thing that won.

## The shared-budget trap

<Warning>
  Raising budget on one ad set inside a campaign with a shared budget re-divides money across everything in that campaign. "Scale the winner" and "starve everything else" are the same action.
</Warning>

Say which campaign a winner sits in, and whether its budget is shared, before touching it.

## What to watch

| Signal | Likely cause |
| --- | --- |
| Cost per order climbs as budget rises | Audience saturating |
| CPM climbs sharply | Competing harder for the same people |
| Frequency climbs | Creative fatigue is coming |
| Account-wide cost per order drifts up | The brake is about to come on |

## Creative fatigue

A winner does not stay a winner. Frequency climbs, response falls, and cost per order drifts back toward the bar.

The answer is the next iteration of the winning idea. This is why iterate sends work back into testing rather than ending it. Scaling and testing run at the same time, permanently.

Start the next iteration while the current winner is still working. By the time fatigue shows in the numbers, you are already behind.

## Feed it back

- Close the test with the verdict in your own words. That writes the learning
- Label the idea with its angle, audience and format
- Iterate deliberately. The next angle off a winning idea is the most valuable thing you can fund
