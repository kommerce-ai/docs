description: "The three outcomes, and closing a test so it teaches something."

Every idea ends in one of three places. Telling them apart correctly is most of the skill in running tests.

| Outcome | Means | What you do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Kill** | Clearly not working, with evidence | Stop. Do not retest without a reason |
| **Iterate** | Something here worked | Make another version of the part that worked |
| **Graduate** | Proven twice | Move it to [scaling](/operator-wiki/performance/scaling) |

## The one people get wrong

"Stop: promising, unproven" is not a kill. The idea ran out of funding while still looking plausible. The evidence is incomplete, not negative.

<Warning>
  Treating promising ideas as failures is the most expensive habit in testing. You throw away ideas you have already part-funded, then spend fresh money discovering something similar. Keep a list.
</Warning>

## What iterate means

Not "make it again, differently". Iterate on the specific thing that worked.

If the hook earned cheap clicks but the body lost them, keep the hook and change the body. If an angle worked for one persona, try it on an adjacent one.

Labels make this possible. Kommerce reads each idea's angle, audience and format when suggesting what to iterate on.

## When to overrule

The engine reads numbers. You know things it does not.

- Stock ran out mid-test, so orders stopped for an unrelated reason
- A promotion ran and inflated a window
- The landing page broke
- A competitor ran an aggressive offer that week

Overruling is legitimate and expected. Record why. The reason is what makes the result interpretable later.

## Closing a test

A test ends as **winner**, **loser** or **inconclusive**, pointing at scale, iterate or stop.

Write the verdict in your own words rather than accepting the draft. The draft knows the numbers. It does not know the client conversation or the stock problem that explains them.

Closing a test writes a learning that every future brief can draw on. This is the step that turns a test from an event into something the account remembers.

Nothing is deleted. Tests get archived. Learnings get superseded.
