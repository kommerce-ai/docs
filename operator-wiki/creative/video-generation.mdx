The video studio is five gated steps. It starts the same way the image studio does — with a brief that produces an angle, hooks, and a CTA — then adds the stages a video needs.

Open **Creative Production** and switch the toggle to **Video**.

## The five steps

| Step | What it decides |
| --- | --- |
| **Brief** | The messaging — angle, hooks, CTA |
| **Style** | The visual treatment |
| **Cast** | Who appears |
| **Script** | The spoken and on-screen copy, built from the hooks |
| **Post-production** | Final assembly |

Each step is gated, and an attempt to jump ahead tells you what is missing rather than failing silently.

<Note>
  Studio state persists. You can leave mid-flow and come back to the step you were on — useful, because video runs are longer than image runs and rarely finish in one sitting.
</Note>

## Step 1 — Brief

The brief moves through phases: first you set context, then you choose how the messaging gets written.

**Context** — pick the product, the persona, and the format.

**Mode** — then choose one of three ways in:

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Generate new" icon="sparkles">
    From product plus brand context. The default path.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Upload Brief" icon="file-arrow-up">
    Drop a `.docx`, `.pdf`, `.md`, or `.txt`. Use when the brief already exists — a client brief, a strategy doc.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Reference Ad" icon="link">
    Drop a reference ad — a video file or a link. Use when you want to work from something that already performs.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

All three converge on the same editable **angle, hooks, and CTA**. Review them here; the script step builds directly on the hooks, so a weak hook propagates.

<Tip>
  **Reference Ad** is the most underused of the three. If a client has a competitor ad they keep pointing at, feeding it in is faster and more faithful than trying to describe what they like in the Theme field.
</Tip>

## Step 2 — Style

The visual treatment for the video. This is where the look is set, independent of who appears in it.

## Step 3 — Cast

Who appears. Decided separately from style so you can change the look without recasting, and recast without redoing the look.

## Step 4 — Script

The script, built from the brief's hooks. Because it derives from the brief, fixing a weak line is usually better done by editing the hook and letting the script follow than by rewriting the script alone.

## Step 5 — Post-production

Final assembly. The studio returns here after a render completes, so it is also where you review output.

## Video versus image

They are not interchangeable, and the cost difference is real:

| | Image | Video |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Steps | 3 | 5 |
| Time per asset | Fast | Substantially slower |
| Credit cost | Lower | Higher per asset |
| Good for | Volume testing across a matrix | Proven angles worth the production |

<Warning>
  Do not use video to test angles. Prove an angle with cheap static variations first, then produce video against the one that worked. Testing at video cost is the fastest way to spend a brand's [credit balance](/operator-wiki/credits) with little to show.
</Warning>

## Shared context

Both studios read the same products, personas, and awareness data. Setting a product up for image work makes it immediately available for video, and vice versa.
