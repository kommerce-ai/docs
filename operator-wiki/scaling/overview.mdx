Scaling is what testing is for. An idea that has survived testing and confirmation gets proposed for graduation, and the engine labels it plainly: **Winner — move it to scale**.

## What "winner" actually means

A graduated arm has cleared a specific bar, not a vibe:

- It passed its testing window at 90% confidence against break-even CPA
- It then held up on a **separate, fresh** confirmation window
- It did both with at least 72 hours and real funding behind it

<Note>
  The confirmation window is what makes graduation trustworthy. An arm that got lucky early passes testing and then fails confirmation — it goes back to testing rather than to scale. By the time something graduates, it has been right twice on different data.
</Note>

## The three next actions

Every concluded test ends with one:

| Action | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `scale` | Proven. Put budget behind it |
| `iterate` | Something worked; make another execution of it |
| `stop` | Nothing here worth continuing |

Only `scale` leaves the testing loop. `iterate` feeds straight back into [test generation](/operator-wiki/testing/test-generation) as the 80% of slots reserved for proven ideas.

## Scaling changes the thing you proved

This is where most scaling goes wrong.

<Warning>
  A winner was proven **in a specific context**: its ad set, its audience, its budget level, its placements. Change several of those at once while raising spend and you are no longer running the thing that won — you are running something new, at a higher budget, without evidence.
</Warning>

Change one dimension at a time and let each one settle:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Raise budget on what already exists">
    Same ad set, same targeting. The cheapest move and the one most likely to hold.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Then widen the audience">
    Once the higher budget is stable.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Then add placements or formats">
    Each is a new context. Treat a format change as an iteration worth briefing, not a free extension.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Budget mode matters more at scale

Raising spend on an ad set inside a CBO campaign re-divides a **shared** budget — so scaling one winner can quietly starve everything else in that campaign.

<Tip>
  Say out loud which campaign a winner sits in and whether it is CBO before raising budget. On a CBO campaign, "scale the winner" and "cut the others" are the same action, whether or not anyone intended the second half.
</Tip>

## The account guard still applies

The engine watches account-level CPM and CPA regardless of how well an individual arm is doing. A winner scaled hard enough to move account CPM past twice the median will trip the guard.

That is the system telling you the scale step outran the account, not that the idea stopped working.

## Feed it back

A graduated winner should leave a trace that outlives the campaign:

- **Conclude the test** with the verdict in your own words — this writes the learning
- **Label the arm** with its angle, persona and format, so future recommendations can find it
- **Iterate deliberately** — a winning idea's next angle or hook takes its own slot, and that is the highest-value slot you have
