Scaling is what testing is for. When an idea has survived both its runs, Kommerce says so plainly: **Winner: move it to scale**.

## What "winner" actually means

A winner has cleared a specific bar, not a vibe:

- It beat break-even CPA in its first run, with the evidence to back it
- It then held up again on a **separate run with fresh spend**
- It did both over at least 72 hours, with real budget behind it

<Note>
  That second run is what makes a winner trustworthy. An idea that got lucky early passes testing and then fails confirmation — it goes back to testing rather than to scale. By the time Kommerce calls something a winner, it's been right twice, on different money.
</Note>

## Where a finished test points

Every concluded test ends with one:

| Next step | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Scale** | Proven. Put budget behind it |
| **Iterate** | Something worked — make another version of it |
| **Stop** | Nothing here worth continuing |

Only **scale** leaves the testing loop. **Iterate** feeds straight back into [test generation](/operator-wiki/testing/test-generation), as most of the room reserved for ideas that already work.

## Scaling changes the thing you proved

This is where most scaling goes wrong.

<Warning>
  A winner was proven **in a specific context**: its ad set, its audience, its budget level, its placements. Change several of those at once while raising spend and you are no longer running the thing that won — you are running something new, at a higher budget, without evidence.
</Warning>

Change one dimension at a time and let each one settle:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Raise budget on what already exists">
    Same ad set, same targeting. The cheapest move and the one most likely to hold.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Then widen the audience">
    Once the higher budget is stable.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Then add placements or formats">
    Each is a new context. Treat a format change as an iteration worth briefing, not a free extension.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Budget mode matters more at scale

Raising spend on an ad set inside a CBO campaign re-divides a **shared** budget — so scaling one winner can quietly starve everything else in that campaign.

<Tip>
  Say out loud which campaign a winner sits in and whether it is CBO before raising budget. On a CBO campaign, "scale the winner" and "cut the others" are the same action, whether or not anyone intended the second half.
</Tip>

## The account guard still applies

Kommerce watches account-wide CPM and cost per order regardless of how well one idea is doing. Scale a winner hard enough to push account CPM to twice its normal level and the brake comes on.

That's telling you the scale step outran the account, not that the idea stopped working.

## Feed it back

A graduated winner should leave a trace that outlives the campaign:

- **Close the test** with the verdict in your own words — that writes the learning
- **Label the idea** with its angle, audience and format, so future suggestions can find it
- **Iterate deliberately** — the next angle off a winning idea is the most valuable thing you can put budget behind
