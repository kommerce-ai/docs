Copy comes from evidence and customers' own language. Not from instinct, and not from how the brand describes itself.

## Read before writing a word

Pull these first and tag every line you might use:

| Source | What it gives you |
| --- | --- |
| **Product review themes** | The richest source. Real phrasing, real objections |
| **Persona pain and objections** | Structured, but a hypothesis until validated |
| **Copy of the winning ads** | What already worked here |
| **Claimable numbers** | What you're allowed to assert |
| **Approved and banned claims** | Hard rules, not preferences |
| **Competitor wording** | Patterns to borrow, phrasing to avoid repeating |

Copy exact phrases. Sort them into pain, desired outcome, objection and rave. Star the vivid ones — those become headlines almost verbatim.

## The headline's job

Set by the awareness level, every time. See [Awareness levels](/operator-wiki/creative-testing/awareness-levels).

## Appeals, in order of pull

John Caples, and still true:

1. **Self-interest** — what she gets
2. **News** — what's changed
3. **Curiosity** — what she doesn't know

<Warning>
  Curiosity alone is weak. It earns a click and loses the sale, which on a cost-per-order bar is worse than no click. Always pair it with a benefit.
</Warning>

"Quick and easy" works alongside any of them.

## Rules that hold regardless

- **Specific beats vague.** "23 minutes faster" beats "sleep better."
- **Her words beat yours.** If reviews say "knocked out", don't write "improved sleep quality".
- **Claims must be claimable.** Approved claims verbatim; banned words absent. In supplements and skincare this is a legal matter, not a stylistic one.
- **Never fake a testimonial.** Only quote a review that's real, attributable, and producible on request.

## Length limits

Meta primary text truncates. Aim for **125 characters or fewer**; the hard limit is 400. Past the fold the reader sees "... See more" instead of your offer.

## Long-form is a different craft

A long-form static — an advertorial or listicle-style ad of 600 to 2,000 words — follows its own structure: pattern-interrupt hook, symptom cascade, named mechanism, ingredient stack, delayed brand introduction, transformation timeline, call to action.

Kommerce has a dedicated Skill for each length. See [Skills](/operator-wiki/resources/skills).

## Varying a batch

Ads competing in the same ad set shouldn't be paraphrases of each other — they'll split the same audience rather than reaching different ones. Vary the lever, not the wording.
