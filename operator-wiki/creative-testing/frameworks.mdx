---
title: "Creative testing frameworks"
description: "Shapes of test that answer a question cleanly, and the trap most test plans fall into."
---

A framework is just a way of making sure the test answers something. The trap is always the same: changing several things at once and learning nothing.

## Hold one thing variable

| Framework | What varies | Everything else |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Angle test** | The claim or positioning | Persona, awareness, format |
| **Awareness test** | Where in the funnel you speak | Angle, persona, format |
| **Persona test** | Who it's aimed at | Angle, awareness, format |
| **Offer test** | The deal | Creative, audience |
| **Format test** | Static vs video vs creator | Message, audience |
| **Funnel test** | The landing page | Ads held identical |

## Matched pairs

The cleanest shape. Two versions share everything — the same image, the same creator, the same session — and differ only in the variable.

Whatever difference you measure is attributable, because nothing else was free to move.

## Writing the hypothesis

A usable hypothesis has four parts:

> We believe **[change]** will **[result]** compared with **[control or break-even]**, because **[reason, with evidence]**. Against it: **[the strongest counter-evidence]**.

That last clause is the one people drop, and it's the one that makes the test honest. If you can't name anything arguing against your idea, you probably haven't read the evidence.

<Note>
  Kommerce stores a hypothesis up to 600 characters. If yours doesn't fit, it's usually testing more than one thing.
</Note>

## Budget shape

Default to **per-ad-set budgets** rather than a shared campaign budget. A shared budget redistributes toward early leaders, which means one version can starve before it produces a read — and you've paid for a test that can't answer its question.

<Warning>
  A shared campaign budget is a scaling tool, not a testing tool. It optimises for immediate return, which is precisely the opposite of what a test is buying.
</Warning>

## Naming

Copy the pattern the brand's live ads already use, changing only the test number, awareness code, slug and version. Consistent names are what make patterns readable later — and patterns are how the account learns.

## Don't re-answer answered questions

Check past tests and learnings before writing. Re-testing something the account already knows is the cheapest mistake to avoid and the easiest to make.
