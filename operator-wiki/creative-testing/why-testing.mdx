---
title: "Why brands need creative testing"
description: "What testing buys that optimisation can't, and the argument to make to a sceptical client."
---

Media buying can only choose between the ads you gave it. Once the account is structured sensibly and tracking works, the creative is what's left — and the only way to find better creative is to test it.

## What testing actually buys

**A bar, not an opinion.** Without a test, "this ad is good" is a judgement. With one, it's a cost per order measured against break-even.

**Permission to spend.** Nobody wants to put serious budget behind an untested idea. A winner that has proven itself twice is something you can scale without arguing about it.

**A memory.** Each concluded test writes a learning the account keeps. Six months in, an account that tested has a body of knowledge; one that just optimised has a spend history.

## The argument for a sceptical client

Most tests lose. Say this first, because it sounds like failure and isn't.

The economics work because losses are capped and wins aren't. An idea that fails stops at a couple of hundred in spend. An idea that wins gets scaled for months. You're buying cheap information about which is which.

<Note>
  The alternative isn't "no losing ads" — it's losing ads discovered slowly, at full budget, without ever learning which part was wrong.
</Note>

## When testing isn't the answer

Testing more creative against a broken funnel is the most expensive mistake available, because it looks like work.

If clicks are cheap and orders don't follow, the problem is after the click — the page, the offer, or the checkout. Kommerce flags this as **fix the funnel** rather than suggesting more ads. Believe it.

<Warning>
  Equally, don't test on an account whose tracking is unreliable. Every verdict inherits the quality of the signal underneath it. Run the [account audit](/operator-wiki/client-success/account-audit) first.
</Warning>
