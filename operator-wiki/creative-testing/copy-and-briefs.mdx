Copy comes from evidence and customers' own language. Not from instinct, and not from how the brand describes itself.

## Read before writing a word

Pull these first and tag every line you might use.

| Source | What it gives you |
| --- | --- |
| Product review themes | The richest source. Real phrasing, real objections |
| Persona pain and objections | Structured, but a hypothesis until validated |
| Copy of the winning ads | What already worked here |
| Claimable numbers | What you are allowed to assert |
| Approved and banned claims | Hard rules, not preferences |
| Competitor wording | Patterns to borrow, phrasing to avoid repeating |

Copy exact phrases. Do not paraphrase them. Sort them into pain, desired outcome, objection and rave. Star the vivid ones.

Starred phrases become headlines almost verbatim. That is the point of the exercise.

## Appeals, in order of pull

1. **Self-interest.** What she gets.
2. **News.** What has changed.
3. **Curiosity.** What she does not know.

"Quick and easy" works alongside any of them.

Curiosity alone is weak. It earns the click and loses the sale. On a cost-per-order bar that is worse than no click.

## Rules that always hold

- Specific beats vague. "23 minutes faster" beats "sleep better".
- Her words beat yours. If reviews say "knocked out", do not write "improved sleep quality".
- Claims must be claimable. Approved claims verbatim, banned words absent.
- Never fake a testimonial. Only quote a real, attributable review the brand can produce.
- Vary the lever, not the wording. Ads in one ad set should not be paraphrases.

<Warning>
  In supplements and skincare, a banned claim reaching a live ad is a legal problem, not a style note. Check the brand's rules before writing, not after.
</Warning>

## Length

Meta primary text truncates. Aim for 125 characters or fewer. The hard limit is 400. Past the fold the reader sees "... See more" instead of your offer.

## Long-form is a different craft

A long-form static runs 600 to 2,000 words and follows a fixed sequence. Pattern-interrupt hook, symptom cascade, named mechanism, ingredient stack, delayed brand introduction, transformation timeline, call to action.

Kommerce has a separate Skill for each length. See [Skills](/operator-wiki/resources/skills).

## Writing the brief

Every idea in a test needs a brief. A brief states what this idea is testing, so that when the verdict arrives you know what was proven.

A brief carries the angle, the hooks and the call to action.

### Change one thing at a time

If an idea changes the angle and the audience and the format, the verdict tells you nothing useful. You will know it won or lost. You will not know which change did it.

Change one thing per idea. Different executions of the same idea belong together, sharing a budget.

### Label it

Kommerce reads each idea's angle, audience and format when it suggests what to iterate on. An unlabelled idea produces vaguer suggestions.

### Layout

A brief does not need to invent a layout. Kommerce ranks templates for the brand. Attaching one makes generation build inside that structure instead of improvising.

### Sign-off

Generating from a brief is the sign-off. There is no separate approval step. Approving a brief ahead of production, or rejecting it, is a separate deliberate action. A rejected brief stays rejected.
