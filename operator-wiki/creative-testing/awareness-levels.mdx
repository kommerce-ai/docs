---
title: "Customer awareness levels"
description: "The five levels, and what each one allows your opening line to say."
---

Eugene Schwartz's insight, and the most useful single idea in direct response: **how much the reader already knows decides what your headline is allowed to say.**

Get this wrong and the ad fails for a reason no amount of design fixes.

## The five levels

| Level | The headline's job | Opens on |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Unaware** | Make her recognise herself or a moment. No product, no problem named yet | A scene, an identity, a story |
| **Problem Aware** | Name the pain in her words, then hint there's a way out | The problem |
| **Solution Aware** | Say why *this kind* of fix beats the others she knows | The mechanism or category |
| **Product Aware** | Prove this product — specifics, proof, differentiation | The product and its proof |
| **Most Aware** | Give the deal and a reason to act now | The offer |

## Why it decides everything

An ad opening on a discount speaks to someone already deciding between brands. Show it to someone who doesn't know they have the problem and it's noise — she has no frame to put the offer in.

The reverse fails too. A long story about recognising a feeling wastes a reader who already has her card out.

<Note>
  Each idea in Kommerce carries exactly one awareness label. **If the opening words don't match that label, either the copy is wrong or the label is wrong.** Fix it before the ad saves — it's the cheapest correction available and nearly free to make at brief stage.
</Note>

## Picking the level

Most accounts are not short of Most Aware ads — retargeting tends to fill that in by itself. The gap is usually higher up the funnel, where you're paying to reach people who don't know the brand.

<Tip>
  Selecting several awareness levels in one test multiplies your output and your budget. Each level is a different idea needing its own spend to produce a verdict. Testing one properly beats testing three thinly.
</Tip>

## A worked contrast

For a supplement that helps sleep:

- **Unaware** — "3pm and you're reading the same sentence again."
- **Problem Aware** — "Still tired after eight hours? It isn't the hours."
- **Solution Aware** — "Not melatonin. A magnesium blend that works with your own cycle."
- **Product Aware** — "4,000 reviews. Average 23 minutes faster to sleep."
- **Most Aware** — "30% off the sleep bundle. Ends Sunday."

Same product, five different first lines — and only one of them is right for a given audience.
