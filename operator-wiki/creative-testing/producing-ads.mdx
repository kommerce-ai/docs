Every test runs in exactly one mode. Format is held constant inside a test. If the strategy calls for two modes, that is two tests asking the same question.

## The four modes

| Mode | Who makes it | Lead time |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Statics** | Designer, or generated | Fastest |
| **Video** | Editor, re-cutting existing footage | Medium |
| **Motion** | Motion designer, from stills and cutouts | Medium |
| **Trybe creators** | Creators sourced through Trybe | Slowest |

## Choosing one

<Steps>
  <Step title="The format the evidence ran in">
    If the winning ad was a static, test the angle as statics first.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Brand rules">
    A brand that bans AI-generated people rules out whole modes before you start.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Timeline">
    Under about five days to launch means statics, or motion and video from existing assets. Creators need casting, shipping and filming.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Budget">
    Video and creators cost more to make. Worth it when the test can fund a real read, or when the creative will be reused afterwards.
  </Step>
</Steps>

State the reason alongside the choice. "Statics, because the current winner is logged as an image and we are two days from launch" is a decision someone can disagree with. "Let's do statics" is not.

## Statics

Three steps: brief, templates, generate.

Pick templates from the **Select Visual Blueprints** grid. Work out what you are about to generate before clicking Create. The count is roughly angles × products × awareness levels × personas × variations. Three angles across three awareness levels, two personas and three variations is 54 assets from one click.

Sizes: 4:5 and 1:1, plus 9:16 if Stories or Reels placements are on.

## Video

Five steps: Brief, Look, Script, Storyboard, Post-production.

The brief works three ways. **Generate new** writes from product and brand context. **Upload brief** takes a `.docx`, `.pdf`, `.md` or `.txt`. **Reference ad** works from an ad you already like, as a file or a link.

Reference ad is the most underused. If a client keeps pointing at a competitor's ad, feeding it in beats describing it.

Script comes before storyboard on purpose. The shots are chosen to carry lines that already exist.

When re-cutting a proven ad, leave the body alone and change only the variable. Usually that is the hook or the end card.

## Motion

The hook frame has to read with sound off inside one second. Five words a frame at most. One movement idea per frame.

Most feed views start muted. A video whose first second only works with sound is a video most people never see.

## Trybe creators

A creator brief is direction, not a script. You are briefing a person who will say it their own way.

| Part | What goes in it |
| --- | --- |
| Profile | Matches the persona |
| Talking points | Customer phrases, in her own words |
| Must say | Approved claims, verbatim |
| Never say | The brand's banned words |

For matched pairs, have one creator film both versions in a single session. That holds the creator constant, so the difference you measure is the variable and not one person being more watchable.

## Generation is quoted before it spends

Asking to generate returns a price. Nothing is spent at that point. It only runs when you confirm.

<Warning>
  The wallet is a hard ceiling. A run that would exceed the balance is refused outright. It is never half-completed, so you will not find part of a matrix rendered and the rest missing.
</Warning>

Renders take about two minutes. Check back rather than waiting.

## Reviewing what came back

Zoom in before judging. Text that looks wrong at thumbnail size is often fine close up.

Then score it, comment on the region at fault, regenerate, and approve or reject.

Comment on the region rather than describing the problem in general terms. A regeneration that knows where the problem is produces a targeted fix. One that only knows "make it better" rerolls the whole asset.

Regeneration replaces the asset in place. There is no version history. Download anything you might want back.
