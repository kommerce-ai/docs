## What a test is

You test **ideas**. An idea is one thing you want to learn about: an angle, an audience, an offer.

Each idea gets its own ad set and its own budget. Different executions of the same idea are **versions**, and they share that ad set.

So five versions of one angle cost one budget. Five different angles cost five budgets.

<Note>
  This is the distinction that decides what a test costs. "Make five more versions" is cheap. "Test five more ideas" is five times the money.
</Note>

## Why brands need it

**It replaces opinion with a number.** Without a test, "this ad is good" is a judgement. With one, it is a cost per order measured against break-even.

**It makes scaling defensible.** Nobody wants real budget behind an untested idea. An idea that has proven itself twice is one you can scale without arguing.

**It gives the account a memory.** Every closed test writes a learning. After six months, an account that tested has a body of knowledge. An account that only optimised has a spend history.

## Most tests lose

Say this to a client before the first losing test, not after it.

The economics work because losses are capped and wins are not. A failing idea stops at a couple of hundred in spend. A winning idea runs for months. You are buying cheap information about which is which.

## When not to test

Testing more creative against a broken funnel is the most expensive mistake available. It also looks like work, which is why it keeps happening.

If clicks are cheap and orders do not follow, the problem is after the click. It is the page, the offer, or the checkout. Kommerce flags this as **fix the funnel** instead of suggesting more ads.

Do not test on an account with unreliable tracking either. Every verdict inherits the quality of the signal underneath it. Run the [account audit](/operator-wiki/client-success/account-audit) first.

## Test shapes

A framework is just a way of making sure the test answers something. Hold one thing variable and keep the rest constant.

| Shape | What varies |
| --- | --- |
| Angle test | The claim or positioning |
| Awareness test | Where in the funnel you speak |
| Persona test | Who it is aimed at |
| Offer test | The deal |
| Format test | Static, video or creator |
| Funnel test | The landing page, with ads held identical |

**Matched pairs** are the cleanest shape. Two versions share everything: the same image, the same creator, the same filming session. Only the variable differs. Any difference you measure is therefore attributable.

## Writing the hypothesis

A usable hypothesis has four parts.

> We believe **[change]** will **[result]** compared with **[control or break-even]**, because **[reason, with evidence]**. Against it: **[strongest counter-evidence]**.

Most people drop the last clause. It is the one that makes the test honest. If nothing argues against your idea, you have not read the evidence properly.

Kommerce stores a hypothesis up to 600 characters. If yours will not fit, it is usually testing more than one thing.

## Do not re-answer answered questions

Check past tests and learnings before you write. Re-testing something the account already established is the easiest money to waste.
