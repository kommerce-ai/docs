This page deliberately contains no invented examples.

A made-up winning test teaches the wrong lesson, because the numbers that made it a winner were never real. The examples worth learning from are the ones your own accounts produced — and Kommerce already keeps them.

## Where the real examples are

| Source | What it holds |
| --- | --- |
| **Learnings** | What concluded tests established, each with how it's known and how confident it is |
| **Past tests** | The full plan, hypothesis, evidence and verdict for every test that ran |
| **Patterns** | Every ad in the account grouped by persona, awareness, angle and page, labelled against break-even |

Read these before briefing. They're the account's memory, and they are specific to the brand in a way a generic case study can never be.

## Reading a concluded test

A closed test is a worked example of the whole process:

<Steps>
  <Step title="The hypothesis">
    What was believed, and what argued against it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The evidence">
    What justified spending money on the question.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The verdict">
    What happened, in the operator's own words — including the context the numbers don't show.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The learning">
    The durable sentence it produced.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  The most instructive tests are usually the **inconclusive** ones. A winner tells you what worked; an inconclusive test usually tells you the question was badly framed — which is the more transferable lesson.
</Note>

## Building a shared example set

Worked examples that generalise across brands belong in the [Kommerce library](https://github.com/kommerce-ai/kommerce-library) rather than here, so they sit beside the skill they illustrate and get reviewed before anyone copies them.

A good candidate example has:

- Real numbers, with spend attached
- The reasoning that led to the test, not just the result
- What was *wrong* about the original hypothesis, where something was

<Warning>
  Anonymise before anything leaves the account. This site is public — a brand name, a spend figure or a ROAS from a real client does not belong on it.
</Warning>

## The lessons that recur

These hold across accounts and are worth knowing before your first test:

- **Most ideas lose.** The economics work because losses are capped and wins aren't.
- **Promising, unproven is not failure.** It's the cheapest retest you'll ever have.
- **More creative won't fix a funnel problem.** Check where the drop-off is first.
- **An optimistic bar is expensive.** It kills profitable ideas and promotes unprofitable ones at the same time.
- **Tests that never close teach nothing.** The verdict is what writes the learning.
