## Awareness decides the opening

Eugene Schwartz's framework is the one that does the most work. What the reader already knows decides what the headline may say — see [Awareness levels](/operator-wiki/creative-testing/awareness-levels).

Applied badly, every other framework underneath it fails.

## Appeals, in order of pull

John Caples, *Tested Advertising Methods*:

1. **Self-interest** — what she gets
2. **News** — what's changed
3. **Curiosity** — what she doesn't know

Plus **quick and easy**, which works alongside any of them.

<Warning>
  Curiosity alone earns the click and loses the sale. On a cost-per-order bar, that's worse than no click at all. Pair it with a benefit.
</Warning>

## Message mining

Copy is found, not invented. Pull real phrases from reviews and support conversations, sort them into **pain**, **desired outcome**, **objection** and **rave**, and star the vivid ones.

Starred phrases become headlines almost verbatim. If reviews say "knocked out", write that — not "improved sleep quality".

## The long-form shape

A 600–2,000 word static follows a fixed sequence:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Pattern-interrupt hook">
    Stops the scroll without naming the product.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Symptom cascade">
    Builds recognition through specifics she'll know.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Named mechanism">
    Why this works, given a name worth remembering.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Ingredient stack">
    The proof behind the mechanism.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Delayed brand introduction">
    The brand arrives once she's already persuaded.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Transformation timeline">
    What happens, and when.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Call to action">
    The offer and the reason to act now.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Auditing copy

The RMBC lens asks where copy fails: **Research**, **Mechanism**, **Brief**, or **Copy execution**.

Most weak copy fails at research — it was written from instinct. Rewriting the execution of a badly researched ad is polishing the wrong thing.

## Rules that survive every framework

- Specific beats vague
- Her words beat yours
- Claims must be claimable — approved verbatim, banned absent
- Never fake a testimonial
- Vary the lever, not the wording, across one ad set
