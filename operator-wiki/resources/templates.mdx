## Hypothesis

> We believe **[change]** will **[result]** compared with **[control or break-even]**, because **[reason, with evidence]**. Against it: **[strongest counter-evidence]**.

Up to 600 characters. If it does not fit, it is usually testing more than one thing.

The "against it" clause is what makes the test honest. If nothing argues against your idea, you have not read the evidence.

## Test plan

| Section | What goes in it |
| --- | --- |
| **Overview** | Two or three sentences. What it tests and the strongest evidence |
| **Brand guidelines** | Five bullets. Voice, look, product on screen, claims, never |
| **Title** | Test number, persona, angle, what is contrasted |
| **Volume** | Ad sets × creatives = total ads, with budget and per-day figure |
| **Hypothesis** | As above |
| **Why** | The business problem in numbers |
| **Evidence** | Three to ten rows. Real numbers with spend, each with a strength |
| **Plan** | Persona, awareness, angle, the variable, what is held constant |
| **Success** | The bar, and the best current ad to beat |
| **Creative briefs** | One row per ad |
| **QA** | Tick only what is true. Open items become blockers |

## Creative brief row

| Field | What it holds |
| --- | --- |
| **Headline and CTA** | Meta headline with character count, then the button |
| **Visual** | The composition. Photo, product's role, where the offer sits |
| **Image copy** | On-image headline, subline, offer line |
| **Lever and reference** | What this version pulls, with links to real ads |
| **Ad copy** | Meta primary text with character count. Aim 125 or fewer, limit 400 |

<Warning>
  Never invent an ad reference. Link to a real ad in the Meta Ad Library or your saved research, or leave it out.
</Warning>

## Creator brief

Profile matching the persona. Talking points in customer phrasing. Must say, with approved claims verbatim. Never say, with banned words.

Direction, not a script. The creator says it their own way.

## Evidence row

> **[Tag]** — what it showed, with real numbers and spend — *strength*

"4 purchases at $32.08 on $128 spend, directional" is evidence. "Converts well" is not.

## Copywriting frameworks

**Awareness decides the opening.** Eugene Schwartz's framework does the most work. What the reader knows decides what the headline may say.

**Appeals, in order of pull.** John Caples: self-interest, news, curiosity. Plus "quick and easy", which works alongside any of them. Curiosity alone earns the click and loses the sale.

**Message mining.** Copy is found, not invented. Pull real phrases from reviews, sort them into pain, desired outcome, objection and rave, and star the vivid ones.

**The long-form shape.** Pattern-interrupt hook, symptom cascade, named mechanism, ingredient stack, delayed brand introduction, transformation timeline, call to action.

**Auditing copy.** The RMBC lens asks where copy fails: Research, Mechanism, Brief, or Copy execution. Most weak copy fails at research. Rewriting the execution of badly researched copy polishes the wrong thing.

## Where real examples live

This page carries no invented examples. A made-up winning test teaches a lesson that was never true.

The examples worth learning from are in the account already: **learnings**, **past tests** and **patterns**. Read a closed test as a worked example of the whole process.

The most instructive tests are usually the inconclusive ones. A winner tells you what worked. An inconclusive test usually tells you the question was badly framed, which transfers further.
