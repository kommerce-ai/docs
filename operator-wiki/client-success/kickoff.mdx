---

Kickoff happens once the brand is **launch ready**: onboarding steps done and the Meta account connected.

## Before the call

<Steps>
  <Step title="Run the account audit">
    Especially on an account with history. You want to know about broken tracking before the client asks why numbers look odd. See [Account audit](/operator-wiki/client-success/account-audit).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check the still-needed list">
    Business verification and payment method have real external latency. If either is outstanding, the launch date you agree on the call is already at risk.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Know the bar">
    Break-even CPA, and whether it's a real figure or an assumed one.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What to set expectations on

**Testing produces verdicts, not instant wins.** Nothing is judged in the first 72 hours, and an idea needs real spend behind it to prove anything. A client expecting day-two results will read a working test as a failing one.

**Most ideas lose. That's the point.** The job is finding the few that win cheaply, not being right first time. Say this before the first losing test, not after it.

**Publishing is not spending.** Everything arrives paused and someone activates it deliberately. Clients find this reassuring when it's explained up front and alarming when they discover it mid-launch.

<Warning>
  Don't promise a launch date off "onboarding complete". That status means the steps are done; it doesn't mean Meta will let the account spend. Check business verification separately — see [Launch readiness](/operator-wiki/client-success/launch-readiness).
</Warning>

## What to agree

- The bar — break-even CPA, and who owns correcting it if it's wrong
- The reporting rhythm and which channel it lands in
- Who approves creative, spend and claims
- How many ideas are in flight at once, which is a budget question more than a creative one
