---
title: "Positioning, products and unit economics"
description: "The three things you must know about a brand before you can brief, test or judge anything."
---

Everything Kommerce does for a brand reads from three things. Get them wrong and every downstream output is wrong in the same direction.

## Positioning

What the brand is, who it is for, and what it says that competitors don't.

Pull it from the brand guide — voice, look, claims you may make, and claims you may never make. The last one matters most: a banned claim that reaches a live ad is a client problem, not a creative note.

<Note>
  Brand rules are enforceable, not advisory. If a brand bans AI-generated people, that rules out whole creative modes before you start briefing — see [UGC and creators](/operator-wiki/creative-testing/ugc-and-creators).
</Note>

## Products

Each product needs a name, a real description, imagery, and its facts — including review themes, which are the single richest source of ad copy you have.

Customers describe the product better than the brand does. Their words become headlines almost verbatim. See [Copywriting](/operator-wiki/creative-testing/copywriting).

## Unit economics

This is the one operators skip and then regret.

| Number | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| **Average order value** | What a typical order is worth |
| **Cost of goods** | What it costs to fulfil |
| **Break-even ROAS** | The return where you stop making money |
| **Break-even CPA** | What an order can cost before it stops making money |

**Break-even CPA is the bar every test is judged against.** If it's wrong, Kommerce will kill ideas that were profitable and promote ideas that weren't.

<Warning>
  If a brand can't give you real unit economics, say so out loud before testing starts. Kommerce will estimate a bar from order value and break-even ROAS and **mark it as assumed** — workable to begin with, but every verdict built on it inherits that uncertainty.
</Warning>

## Where this goes

All three live on the brand record, and every brief, test and report reads from them. Updating the brand guide is not admin — it's the highest-leverage edit available to you.
