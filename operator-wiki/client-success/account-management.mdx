Once tests are running, the work becomes a loop rather than a project.

## The weekly rhythm

<Steps>
  <Step title="Read the daily pulse">
    Scorecard, what the team is on, ranked suggestions. Anything Red gets dealt with before anything else.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Read the day's test calls">
    Approve what makes sense, overrule what you know better. See [Monitoring tests](/operator-wiki/performance/monitoring-tests).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Keep ideas in flight">
    An account with nothing testing is an account learning nothing. See [Generating tests](/operator-wiki/creative-testing/generating-tests).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Close what's finished">
    A concluded test writes a learning. A test left open writes nothing.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What to watch for

| Signal | What it usually means |
| --- | --- |
| Pulse Red on Creative | The pool has gone stale — same ads carrying spend too long |
| Pulse Red on Efficiency | Spend isn't producing orders; check the audit before the creative |
| No tests closing | Verdicts are being reached and not written up. The account stops accumulating knowledge |
| Every test exploratory | Nobody is reading patterns and learnings before briefing |

<Note>
  The account's memory is its **learnings** and **patterns**. An account where tests run but never close has no memory — you'll re-test answered questions and pay for the answer twice.
</Note>

## Keeping the brand record current

Positioning drifts, products launch, offers change. When Kenny learns something new about a brand, accept it into the brand context — it improves every brief afterwards. Declining just means repeating yourself.
