Set this up in the first week. Communication that gets invented per-request is where delivery quality leaks.

## Channels

Each brand gets a member-only Slack channel when you add it, along with a ClickUp space and a Drive folder. That channel is the brand's thread — client questions, approvals, and the recurring report all land there.

<Warning>
  Slack returns nothing for a channel the bot isn't in, and the report degrades to a quiet note rather than raising an error. A silent daily pulse is almost always this. Invite the bot, then re-verify.
</Warning>

## The reporting rhythm

Kommerce writes a recurring account report — the **daily pulse** — from Meta performance, Slack discussion and ClickUp tasks. It scores the account Green, Yellow or Red across four areas:

| Area | What it watches |
| --- | --- |
| **Activity** | Is work actually happening |
| **Efficiency** | Is spend producing orders |
| **Creative** | Is the creative pool fresh and varied |
| **Coverage** | Are the bases covered |

It also summarises what the team is working on, sets an agenda, and ranks suggestions.

<Tip>
  Read the pulse before the client does. Arriving at a call having already seen a Red is the difference between managing an account and reacting to it.
</Tip>

## Approvals

Agree up front who signs off on what:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Creative">
    Who approves ads before they publish. Kommerce records approve/reject per asset, so this is tracked rather than remembered.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Spend">
    Who may raise budget, and by how much, without asking. Publishing never spends — someone always activates — so this is about who that someone is.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Claims">
    Who signs off anything that makes a factual claim. For supplements and skincare this is not optional.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Every action is attributed to a person, to Kenny or to the system, so "who approved this" is answerable after the fact.
