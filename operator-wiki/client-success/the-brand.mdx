Everything Kommerce produces reads from three things. Get them wrong and every downstream output is wrong in the same direction.

## Positioning

What the brand is, who it is for, and what it says that competitors do not.

Pull it from the brand guide: voice, look, claims you may make, and claims you may never make. The last one matters most. A banned claim reaching a live ad is a client problem, not a creative note.

Brand rules are enforceable, not advisory. A brand that bans AI-generated people has ruled out whole creative modes before you start briefing.

## Products

Each product needs a name, a real description, imagery, and its facts.

Review themes are the richest source of ad copy you have. Customers describe the product better than the brand does, and their words become headlines almost verbatim.

A product with a one-line description and no photos produces weak creative no matter how good the brief is. Generation has nothing to work with.

## Unit economics

This is the one operators skip and then regret.

| Number | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| **AOV** | Average order value |
| **COGS** | What it costs to fulfil an order |
| **Break-even ROAS** | The return where you stop making money |
| **Break-even CPA** | What an order can cost before it stops making money |

Break-even CPA is the bar every test is judged against.

### Where Kommerce gets the bar

In order of preference: a figure frozen on the test, then the brand's unit economics, then an estimate from order value and break-even ROAS.

An estimated bar is **marked as assumed**. It is not wrong, but every verdict built on it inherits that uncertainty. Check before you trust one.

### Why an optimistic bar is expensive

Break-even is the point where an order stops making money. The client's target sits below it. That gap is the margin they want.

Give Kommerce the break-even number, not the target. Two things go wrong otherwise, both invisible. Ideas that were profitable get killed for missing a bar they never had to clear. And the confidence calculations are measured against the wrong line, so "passed" means less than it looks.

<Warning>
  The second problem is worse. It produces winners that do not scale, and you find out after putting real budget behind one.
</Warning>

If a client will not give you real COGS, ask for a range and use the pessimistic end. A cautious bar costs you some good ideas. An optimistic one costs you the account's credibility.

## Keeping it current

Positioning drifts, products launch, offers change. When Kenny learns something new about a brand, accept it into the brand context. It improves every brief afterwards.
