Kenny is the agent behind Kommerce's chat surfaces. He is not a help bot — he can read brand context, generate creative, build campaigns, pull live performance data, and launch to Meta.

Kenny is available to **operator and brand users alike**. What differs is the surrounding workspace, not the agent.

## What he can do

- Read and analyse brand context — profile, personas, products, brand guide
- Create and edit campaigns, ad sets, and creative assets
- Launch campaigns to Meta Ads Manager and Google Ads
- Research competitors and generate marketing insights
- Generate and refine ad copy and headlines
- Fetch live performance data from connected platforms
- Work with a connected Shopify store

Home is chat-first for both roles — you describe what you want rather than finding the right page first.

## He can see the page you're on

This is the part worth understanding, because it changes how you should phrase things.

Each page registers a description of what is currently on screen, and Kenny takes a **fresh snapshot at the moment you send** — not when you opened the chat. So "why is this one underperforming?" while looking at a campaign works, and the answer is grounded in that campaign's actual current data rather than a guess.

Two practical consequences:

- **Open Kenny from the page you're asking about.** Asking about a campaign from the settings page gives him less to work with.
- **Suggested starters change per page.** When the chat is empty, the prompts offered are the ones that make sense where you are.

## How he works underneath

Kenny has two kinds of tools, and knowing which is in play explains his behaviour:

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Direct API tools" icon="bolt">
    Reading live data and taking single actions — Meta, Shopify, Google. Fast and atomic.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Specialist sub-agents" icon="users-gear">
    Multi-step work — campaign creation, creative generation. Slower, because it's real orchestration.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

A request like "what's my ROAS this week" is a direct call. "Build me a campaign for the summer launch" hands off to a specialist and takes correspondingly longer. A pause on the second kind is the system working, not hanging.

## He offers to remember things

When Kenny learns something new about a brand — a persona, a preference, a pain point — he says so and offers to save it to the brand context.

<Tip>
  Accept these when they're right. Brand context is what every brief, report, and audit reads from, so a fact saved once improves output everywhere afterwards. Declining just means repeating yourself next session.
</Tip>

## When to use Kenny instead of a page

**Kenny is better for:** questions spanning several surfaces, "why" questions about performance, quick one-off generation, and anything where you don't know which page you need.

**Pages are better for:** high-volume creative through the [studios](/operator-wiki/creative/overview), careful campaign configuration, and anything where you want to see every field before committing.

<Note>
  Kenny can launch campaigns, but publishing through the [campaign wizard](/operator-wiki/media-buying) shows you budget mode, targeting, and per-ad approval explicitly. For a client's first campaign on a new account, use the wizard.
</Note>

## Limits

- Kenny works within one brand's context at a time. Portfolio-wide questions need the brand switched first.
- He cannot change operator-level settings or integrations for you.
- Anything he launches to Meta arrives **paused**, exactly as the wizard does. Publishing is never spending.
