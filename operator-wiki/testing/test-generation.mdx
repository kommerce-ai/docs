title: "Test generation"
description: "Getting Kommerce to tell you what to test next, and turning that into a test you can launch."
---

You don't start from a blank page. Kommerce reads the account and tells you what to test next, with the numbers behind it.

## Ask for suggestions

Ask for a recommendation and you'll get back a short list. Each one names the idea, what to do about it, and the evidence.

Recommendations come in six flavours:

| Recommendation | What it's telling you |
| --- | --- |
| **Iterate** | Something is working — make another version of it |
| **New concepts** | You don't have enough different ideas in flight |
| **Fix the funnel** | The ads are fine; the problem is after the click |
| **Scale** | An idea has proven itself — move it out of testing |
| **Hold** | Not enough evidence yet. Leave it alone |
| **Drop** | Stop spending on this |

<Note>
  Every recommendation comes with the numbers that produced it. Read those before acting — a recommendation is a rule firing on data, not a judgement about your strategy. You may well know something it doesn't.
</Note>

## Why most new budget goes to ideas that already work

When Kommerce allocates room for new ideas, it puts roughly **four out of five** into variations of things already passing or promising, and **one in five** into genuinely new concepts.

That ratio is deliberate. The most common way testing goes wrong is abandoning something that was working to chase a new idea, then never getting enough evidence on either.

<Tip>
  You can override this for a brand where it doesn't fit — just do it knowingly. Squeezing more out of a half-proven winner usually beats a fresh unknown.
</Tip>

## The recommendation people skip

**Fix the funnel** is the one worth paying attention to.

When clicks are cheap but orders don't follow, more creative won't help — the problem is the page, the offer, or the checkout. Kommerce says so rather than suggesting more ads.

<Warning>
  Generating more creative against a page that isn't converting is the most expensive mistake available in this product, because it looks like productive work. You'll spend real money and real time and the number won't move.
</Warning>

## Turning it into a test

A recommendation becomes a **draft**. Nothing is live yet. Before launching, check:

<Steps>
  <Step title="The ideas">
    One ad set and one budget each. This is the real cost of the test.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The budget">
    Each idea gets about two and a half times break-even CPA. Kommerce also holds back enough to *prove* whatever passes — so it will refuse to launch an idea it couldn't afford to confirm.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The bar">
    Break-even CPA. Everything downstream is judged against it, so get it right here.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  There are two deliberate gaps between a suggestion and money moving: a draft isn't running, and publishing creates everything **paused**.
</Note>

## Already have ad sets running?

You can point a test at ad sets that are already live instead of launching new ones. Kommerce reads them as ideas and applies the same rules.

This is the fastest way to get value from an account you've just taken over — you get verdicts on what's already there without spending anything new.
