You do not start from a blank page. The engine reads the account and says what to test next, with the evidence attached.

## Getting recommendations

Ask for suggestions, or ask the engine to recommend a specific next test. Each recommendation names the arm, what to do, and why.

Recommendations come in six kinds:

| Kind | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| `iterate` | An idea is working — make another execution of it |
| `new_concepts` | Not enough distinct ideas in flight |
| `fix_funnel` | The ad is fine, the problem is downstream |
| `scale` | An idea has proven itself; move it out of testing |
| `hold` | Not enough evidence yet — leave it alone |
| `drop` | Stop spending on this |

<Note>
  Every recommendation carries its **evidence** — the numbers that produced it. Read the evidence before acting. A recommendation is a rule firing on data, not a judgement about your strategy.
</Note>

## The 80/20 split

New slots are allocated deliberately:

- **80%** to iterations of ideas that are passing or promising
- **20%** to genuinely new concepts

This is the engine's answer to the most common way testing goes wrong — abandoning something that was working to chase a new idea, then never getting enough evidence on either.

<Tip>
  If you disagree with the split for a particular brand, that is a strategy call and you can override it. But do it knowingly. The default exists because exploiting a half-proven winner usually beats exploring a fresh unknown.
</Tip>

## `fix_funnel` is the one people skip

When an arm's clicks are cheap but orders do not follow, the problem is not the creative. The engine says so rather than recommending more creative against a page that is not converting.

Generating ten more ads against a broken funnel is the most expensive mistake available in this product, because it looks like work.

## Creating the test

A recommendation becomes a **draft** test. Before anything launches, review:

<Steps>
  <Step title="The versions">
    One per idea. Each one takes a slot and its own funding.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The budget">
    Funding per idea defaults to 2.5 × `C`. The engine reserves enough budget to *confirm* whatever passes — so it will decline to launch an idea it could not afford to prove.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The bar">
    Break-even CPA. Everything downstream is measured against it.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Warning>
  A draft test is not running. Nothing spends until it is published, and publishing creates everything **paused**. Two deliberate gaps between "the engine suggested this" and "money is moving".
</Warning>

## Reading an existing account instead

If ad sets are already running, you can link them to a test rather than launching new ones. The engine then reads them as arms and applies the same rules — useful for taking over an account that already has history.
