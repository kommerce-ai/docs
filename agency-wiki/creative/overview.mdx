Creative production runs through two studios that share the same foundation. Both are **brief-first**: messaging is decided and reviewed before anything renders.

Open **Creative Production** in the sidebar for the brand. The Image/Video toggle sits at the top of the studio.

## What the studios share

Both read the same brand context — products, personas, awareness levels — so a product you set up in one carries to the other.

More importantly, both briefs produce the **same object**: an editable **angle, hooks, and CTA**. That is the unit of messaging in Kommerce. Whether it ends up as a static image or a 30-second video is a downstream decision.

<Note>
  This is why the brief step is worth slowing down on in either studio. The angle is the thing that determines whether creative works; the template or the cast is execution.
</Note>

## Step gating

Both studios gate their steps. You cannot pick templates before generating a brief, and you cannot render before selecting templates. Attempting to skip shows a hint telling you what is missing.

The gating exists because the failure mode it prevents is expensive: firing a large matrix off an unreviewed brief produces volume you then throw away, having already spent [credits](/agency-wiki/credits) on it.

## Where assets land

Finished assets go to the brand's creative library, reachable from **Creative Production** and from the library surface. From there they feed [media buying](/agency-wiki/media-buying) — the campaign wizard's first step selects from this library.

## Reviewing output

Open an individual asset to inspect it and regenerate if it missed.

<Warning>
  Regeneration replaces the asset in place. There is no version history and no rollback — if you might want an asset back, download it before regenerating.
</Warning>

## Briefs in chat

There is also a conversational brief surface, useful when you are still deciding what to make rather than producing at volume. [Kenny](/agency-wiki/kenny) can start creative work directly too — **Create image** is one of his quick actions.

## Where quality actually comes from

When creative reads generically, the cause is almost never the studio. In order of likelihood:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Thin brand guide">
    A vague voice description produces vague copy regardless of how specific the brief is.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Weak product data">
    A one-line description and no imagery gives generation nothing to build on.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Over-broad brief">
    Every awareness level, every persona, high variation count. Broad in, broad out.
  </Step>
</Steps>
