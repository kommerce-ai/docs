The brand guide is the single most important thing in your workspace. Angle research, creative briefs, and reports all read from it. Time spent here improves everything downstream; time spent re-running a brief against a thin guide does not.

Open **My Brand → Brand Guide**.

## What it holds

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Brand voice" icon="microphone">
    How your brand sounds. Written as description, not a list of adjectives.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Communication do's" icon="check">
    One rule per line. Concrete instructions, not aspirations.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Products" icon="box">
    Names, descriptions, and imagery. What creative gets built around.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Identity and context" icon="building">
    Your ICP, competitors, and business details.
  </Card>

Much of this is prefilled from your website during onboarding. Prefilled content is a draft for you to correct, not a finished guide — the scrape gets the shape right and the specifics wrong.

## Writing a voice description that works

The voice field is free text and it is used literally. Vague input produces vague output.

**Weak:** *"Friendly, professional, and approachable."*

That describes most brands and steers nothing.

**Strong:** *"Direct and unfussy. We explain the science without lecturing. Short sentences. We never claim results we cannot cite, and we never use urgency language like 'act now'."*

That second version makes real decisions for the writer — sentence length, what to avoid, how to handle claims.

<Tip>
  The most useful thing you can add is what you *don't* do. Prohibitions steer generated copy harder than positive descriptions, because they rule options out rather than gesturing at a style.
</Tip>

## Communication do's

One per line. Treat these as rules a new copywriter would follow on their first day:

- Use "members", never "customers"
- Always lead with the ingredient, not the outcome
- Never mention competitors by name
- Prices always shown with currency symbol

## Products

Each product carries a name, description, and imagery. Creative briefs ask you to pick a product, and generation builds around its images.

Products with no imagery or a one-line description will produce weak creative regardless of how good the brief is.

## The brand guide assistant

There is a chat assistant on the page. Use it to draft a voice description from examples of copy you like, to turn scattered notes into structured rules, or to check whether a piece of copy actually follows your own guide.

## Keeping it current

Update the guide when your positioning shifts, you launch a product, or you notice generated copy repeatedly making the same mistake.

<Note>
  If you find yourself correcting the same thing in every generated asset, that correction belongs in the brand guide as a rule. Fixing it once upstream beats fixing it every time downstream.
</Note>
