This step captures who you are and how you sound. It has more influence on output quality than any other part of onboarding, because every piece of copy Kommerce writes reads from it.

## What it covers

- **Identity** — what the brand is and what it stands for
- **Voice** — how you sound
- **Your customer** — who you sell to
- **Competitors** — who you're measured against
- **Budget** — the scale you're operating at

## Voice is the field that matters

Voice is free text and it is used literally. Vague input produces vague ads.

**Too vague to be useful:**

> Friendly, professional, and approachable.

That describes almost every brand and steers nothing.

**Actually useful:**

> Direct and unfussy. We explain the science without lecturing. Short sentences. We never claim results we can't cite, and we never use urgency language like "act now".

The second version makes real decisions for a writer — sentence length, what to avoid, how to handle claims.

<Tip>
  The highest-value thing you can add is what you **don't** do. Prohibitions steer generated copy far harder than positive descriptions, because they rule options out instead of gesturing at a style. "We never use countdown timers" does more work than "we're trustworthy".
</Tip>

## Competitors

Name real competitors, not aspirational ones. This field feeds positioning research, which looks for the gap between how you talk and how they talk. Listing a category leader you don't actually compete with produces a gap analysis about a fight you're not in.

## Your customer

Be specific about who actually buys, not who you wish bought. This becomes the personas that creative briefs are written against, and a persona defined too broadly produces copy that lands with nobody.

## If it was drafted for you

A prefilled version came from reading your website. Check these in particular:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Voice">
    Website copy and ad copy are different registers. The scrape captures your site's voice, which is usually more formal than what performs in a feed.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Competitors">
    Rarely on your website at all. Usually needs adding from scratch.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Your customer">
    Sites describe products more than buyers. This is often the thinnest part of a draft.
  </Step>
</Steps>

You can accept a draft as-is, but the three above are worth a pass — they are the ones scraping consistently gets wrong.
